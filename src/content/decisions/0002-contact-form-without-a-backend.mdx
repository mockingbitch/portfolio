## Context

The site is fully static (ADR-0001), so there is no endpoint that can receive a form submission. A contact form is still useful: it gives a visitor a structured way to start a conversation.

Any form that accepts submissions also accepts spam, stores personal data, and becomes something that can silently stop working.

## Decision

The form validates its fields in the browser and then opens a `mailto:` link with the subject and body filled in. The visitor sends the message from their own email client. The page says so next to the button, and the email address is always shown as a plain link.

## Alternatives considered

- **A third-party form service.** Works without a server, but adds an external dependency, sends visitor data to another company, and usually needs spam filtering on top.
- **A serverless function with an email API.** Keeps control of the data, but introduces secrets, rate limiting, abuse handling and delivery monitoring for a low-volume form.
- **No form, only an address.** The simplest option, and still the fallback; the form exists only to lower the effort of writing a first message.

## Consequences

- No visitor data is collected, stored or transmitted by the site.
- There is nothing to operate and no spam endpoint to defend.
- The form depends on the visitor having a configured mail client. Where that is not the case, the visible email address is the fallback.
- If volume or requirements change, the submit handler is the only part that needs replacing.
